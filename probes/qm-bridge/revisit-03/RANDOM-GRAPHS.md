# Why random graphs rather than Hamilton cycles?

4 November 2026 · conceptual source comparison, not an EHF probe result

Carlo A. Trugenberger's model does not give equal weight to arbitrary unconstrained graphs. In the reviewed formulation, he sums over incompressible even-regular graphs: each vertex has the same specified even degree, and short cycles (triangles, squares and pentagons) cannot overlap on more than one edge. A statistical weight exp(-H/g), with H based on Ollivier–Ricci curvature, biases the ensemble. The programme asks whether a geometric phase appears through loop condensation.

Source: C. A. Trugenberger, *Combinatorial quantum gravity and emergent 3D quantum behaviour* (2023), arXiv:2311.17526v1, introduction and “Combinatorial quantum gravity” sections, equations (1)–(3), https://arxiv.org/pdf/2311.17526. These sections and the abstract were inspected for this comparison; no new reproduction of its phase-transition, continuum-limit or quantum-emergence claims was performed. The paper is referenced, not redistributed here.

## Three different graph objects

| Object | Vertices and edges mean | Structure being supplied |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Trugenberger graph configuration | Abstract sites and their yes/no connections | Regular degree, admissibility restrictions and curvature weight; a ring order is not prescribed. |
| One EHF Hamilton cycle | Labelled sites joined in a single cyclic tour | Connectivity, degree two and a cyclic ordering; MICRO/type constraints may add further data. |
| EHF reversal configuration graph | Vertices are whole admissible Hamilton cycles; edges are allowed reversals | A higher-level transition space, with components and loops distinct from the site graph. |

A single cycle C_n has degree two and one-dimensional ring connectivity. It has no short site cycles for n>5. Loops in the graph of cycle rearrangements are different objects and can exist even though an individual site cycle has none of those short loops. Any map between these levels, or identification of one of them with physical geometry, needs to be specified and tested.

Our inference about the modelling tradeoff is that his broader graph ensemble lets connectivity and local loop structure vary while asking whether an ordered geometric phase is selected. Choosing a Hamilton cycle at the outset supplies strong organization and restricts what must emerge. That may make an alternative model tractable, but it also changes the emergence question. His construction supplies assumptions too: random graphs are not assumption-free, and 'random' does not by itself explain dynamics or geometry.

Random and Hamiltonian are not opposites. One can sample Hamilton cycles randomly, sample larger graphs conditioned to contain a Hamilton cycle, or form graphs from several such cycles. The difficulty is choosing the ensemble and its measure: a graph with several Hamilton cycles can acquire different weight if each distinguished cycle is counted as a separate configuration. Those alternatives have not been compared numerically in this probe.

For EHF, the defensible question is whether Hamilton cycles and admissible rearrangements provide a smaller set of assumptions from which useful effective structure can be derived, and whether they miss behaviour a broader graph ensemble supports. Neither the choice of graph class nor a resemblance of vocabulary answers that question.
