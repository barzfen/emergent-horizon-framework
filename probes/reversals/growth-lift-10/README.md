# EHF-GROW-LIFT-02 — Local admissible Hamiltonian orbit lifting

**Date:** 2026-11-13 (AEST)  
**Status:** finite exact enumeration (n=5,7,9), independent positional-swap audit, and an elementary all-n construction. **Not a novel theorem claim, a Collatz result, or a physical interpretation.**  
**Research direction:** Barry Fensom; computation and writing assisted by ChatGPT.

## Question

Can EHF's *actual* Hamiltonian reversal moves, not an arbitrary permutation of insertion histories, define compatible deterministic dynamics across growth levels? Can the admissible move set determine whether a cyclic orbit splits or elongates as new states are added?

## Model, rules, and projections

- Distinct labelled vertices 0,...,n-1; cycles considered modulo cyclic rotation and reflection, canonically anchored at 0 with the lexicographically smaller orientation.
- Fixed binary colours: vertices `R={1,2,3}` are marked 1; all other labels are 0. MICRO means no isolated bit: every vertex has a same-colour cyclic neighbour.
- These states consist of **one block of three marked labels** and **one block of n-3 zero labels**, for n>=5. Their total number is `|X_n|=3!(n-3)!/2 = 3(n-3)!`.
- Growth inserts new zero labels, each into a MICRO-admissible edge. Deletion of the newest unmarked vertex is the inverse parent map. Every state has **n-2** admissible children: n-4 internal edges of the zero run plus two colour-boundary edges. Consequently `X_{n+2} -> X_n` has constant fiber size `(n-2)(n-1)`.
- For odd n, `Pi(C)` is the set of labels at even slots in the canonical representation. OPW3 allows circular LR3 reversals preserving MICRO and Pi; OPW23 additionally allows LR2.
- A deterministic controller `F_n` selects **one** outgoing move at each state; the full OPW3/OPW23 graphs are otherwise nondeterministic. Compatibility demands `pi F_{n+2}=F_n pi`.

## Construction: a truly local, compatible split

Define `F_n(C)` to reverse the **three consecutive marked vertices** (swap its first and third labels). It is always a legal LR3 move. It preserves MICRO, and for odd n preserves Pi (endpoints two slots apart; the canonical orientation rooted at zero does not change because marked labels 1,2,3 are all smaller than the other zero labels). Deletion of newly added *zero* vertices commutes with this flip. The map is an involution with no fixed states.

Therefore there are exactly `3(n-3)!/2` length-2 orbits at every odd size n>=5, and **each parent orbit splits into (n-2)(n-1) length-2 orbits** under n->n+2 growth. This is a valid orbit lift of the *selected deterministic rule*. It does not claim that deletion is a graph morphism for **every** admissible LR3 move (our preceding GROW-LIFT-01 showed it is not).

|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 5 | 6 | 3 | 3 | 3 |
| 7 | 72 | 36 | 54 | 84 |
| 9 | 2,160 | 1,080 | 3,000 | 4,080 |

## OPW23: a selected local four-step orbit above a two-step base

For each of the three OPW3 edges on X5, the two corresponding fibers in X7 have 12 states each.

- The allowed OPW3 **cross-fiber** graph is exactly 12 disjoint edges: a unique perfect matching generated by marked-triple reversal.
- The OPW23 cross-fiber graph has 14 edges: ten isolated pairs plus one 4-cycle (`K_{2,2}`). The extra two edges use LR2, and all edges preserve MICRO and Pi.
- Use the marked-flip matching on the first direction of each base two-cycle; on the reverse direction, switch to the alternate matching only on the single square. This defines a bijective deterministic permutation `F_7` with **30 orbits of length 2 and 3 orbits of length 4** (72 states total), commuting with projection to `F_5`.
- Exactly six of the 72 directed selected moves differ from the marked-flip rule; they are valid LR2 moves. The others are valid LR3. This is an **existence construction with a globally specified selection policy**, not a demonstration of a controller choosing the route using purely local information.

A concrete length-4 orbit (one of three) is:

```text
(0,4,5,1,2,3,6)
  --LR3--> (0,4,5,3,2,1,6)
  --LR2--> (0,4,6,1,2,3,5)
  --LR3--> (0,4,6,3,2,1,5)
  --LR2--> (0,4,5,1,2,3,6)
```

All four cycles have Pi={0,2,5,6}. Their two-step-deletion images alternate between the two five-vertex parent states (0,1,2,3,4) and (0,3,2,1,4).

## Obstruction: the selected OPW23 twist cannot lift locally to size 9

The 7->9 projection has 30 admissible children over *each* X7 state. For each of the six selected LR2 arcs of the twisted F7, the X9 OPW23 graph has only **20** cross-fiber edges, touching just **20 of 30** source children. The other **10 have no admissible LR2/LR3 successor projecting to that prescribed parent target**.

I also tested **both orientations of all six extra LR2 cross-fiber connections** (twelve directed arcs). Every such directed alternative has exactly 20 reachable source children and 10 with no admissible successor projecting to the target.

Thus there is **no everywhere-defined OPW23 local-move permutation F9** satisfying `pi F9=F7_twist pi` for the chosen twist. More strongly, **given this fixed X5 marked-flip base permutation, any single-step OPW23 controller F7 that commutes with X5 and extends as an everywhere-defined single-step OPW23 controller to X9 must equal the marked-triple flip at X7**. To see this, every cross-fiber move at X7 is either the unique LR3 matching or one of the six alternative LR2 edges; all alternative LR2 directions have the demonstrated X9 obstruction. The marked-triple flip does extend to size 9 and beyond.

This is a **bounded, conditional uniqueness/obstruction result** for fixed colour assignments, Pi constraints, two-vertex growth projections, and one permitted reversal per step. It does not rule out other base dynamics, changed constraints, longer microstep protocols, auxiliary controller memory, or nontrivial monodromy in other EHF systems.

## A cocycle/holonomy description

For a base orbit `x -> Fx -> ... -> F^(L-1)x -> x`, write a compatible lift as

`(x,a) -> (Fx, g_x(a))`, with `g_x` a bijection between fibers.

The return permutation `G = g_(F^(L-1)x) ... g_(Fx) g_x` determines lifted cycles: each `q`-cycle of `G` yields one `Lq`-cycle upstairs. For a cyclic shift on a uniform `r`-element fiber by total `s`, this gives `gcd(r,s)` lifted cycles of length `L*r/gcd(r,s)`.

- OPW3 marked flip: return permutation is the identity on each 12-element fiber — 12 independent length-2 orbits.
- Selected OPW23 twist: return permutation has 10 fixed points and one transposition — 10 length-2 orbits and one length-4 orbit above each base pair.

This is standard finite-fiber permutation cocycle/monodromy mathematics, here instantiated with explicit EHF moves. It gives a concrete, disciplined interpretation of the proposed term **Hamiltonian cocycle**. The selected cocycle is not a canonical invariant of all possible EHF dynamics.

## Reproducibility

Run `python3 probe.py` and `python3 verify.py`. Both use only the Python standard library. The primary probe enumerates all admissible states and LR2/LR3 edges for n=5,7,9, assembles the compatible permutations and counts their orbits, checks all commuting projection identities, and enumerates all six 7->9 lift obstructions. Verification uses a separate direct-endpoint-swap generator. The enumeration is exact on these finite carriers; all-size results in this document use the stated simple combinatorial arguments.
