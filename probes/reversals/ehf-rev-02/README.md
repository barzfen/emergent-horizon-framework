# EHF-REV-02 — Exact algebra of a two-reversal interference square

**Date:** 2026-11-19 (Australia/Brisbane)  
**Status:** Exact symbolic result; limited to the CROSS-48 four-state square.

## Question
Does the two-reversal Clifford/Pin interference square already force three inequivalent invariant sectors or the SU(3) 1:2:3 projected squared-norm law?

## Definition
In the ordered basis 00, 01, 10, 11, P flips the first bit and Q flips the second bit with a minus sign when the first bit is one, as specified in [CROSS-48](../../../research/cbb-binary/CROSS-48-reversal-interference.md). Both are signed permutation matrices. They satisfy P²=Q²=I and PQ=-QP.

## Exact computation
[Verifier](probe.py) uses SymPy exact integer linear algebra. It checks:

- Rank of span {I,P,Q,PQ}: **4**.
- Commutant of P and Q inside 4×4 matrices: **dimension 4**.
- Eigenvalues of each of P,Q: +1 with multiplicity 2 and -1 with multiplicity 2.
- Anticommutator PQ+QP: exactly zero.

Over C, the unital algebra generated by P,Q is isomorphic to M₂(C), the complex Clifford algebra on two generators with these relations. The four-dimensional module is two copies of the unique two-dimensional irreducible module. The two copies are equivalent and their particular splitting is not canonical; the commutant permits multiplicity-space mixing.

## Conclusion
**Negative for the minimal hypothesis:** the supplied two-reversal interference square alone does not yield three inequivalent sectors, SU(3), or a 1:2:3 norm law. It supplies an example of coherent interference only once a signed lift and amplitude rule are chosen. This does **not** rule out three-sector behaviour for larger reversal-path systems or additional natural operators.
