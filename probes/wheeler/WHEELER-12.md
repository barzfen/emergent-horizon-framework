# WHEELER-12 — Quotient Uniqueness / Observer-Independence Audit

**Status: PARTIAL PASS — invariant structure is robust across additive local observers; quotient is not unique**

## Question

WHEELER-11 used the observer quotient

`r_i = c_i mod m`

for microscopic oriented boundary-crossing counts `c_i in Z`. Is this a specially tuned encoding, or does the same conserved current structure survive a wider class of natural observer coarse-grainings?

## Microscopic update

An elementary local reversal induces the integer boundary update

`c -> c + e_i - e_{i+1}`,

where `e_i` is the unit vector at boundary `i`.

Therefore the microscopic total

`C = sum_i c_i`

is conserved.

## General additive local observer

Let an observer map each microscopic boundary count into an abelian label group `G` through a homomorphism

`phi: Z -> G`.

Write

`q_i = phi(c_i)`.

Because `phi` is additive,

`phi(c_i + 1) = q_i + g`,

`phi(c_{i+1} - 1) = q_{i+1} - g`,

where

`g = phi(1)`.

Hence every elementary reversal is seen by the observer as

`(q_i, q_{i+1}) -> (q_i + g, q_{i+1} - g)`.

So the observer-level total

`Q = sum_i q_i`

is conserved in `G`.

This is independent of the particular additive quotient chosen.

## Classification of additive local quotients

Since every homomorphism from `Z` is determined by the image of `1`, the observer-visible subgroup generated by `g = phi(1)` is cyclic.

Therefore the effective observer label group is either

- infinite cyclic, isomorphic to `Z`, or
- finite cyclic, isomorphic to `Z_m` for some `m`.

Thus the `mod m` construction used in WHEELER-11 is not an isolated choice. It is the general finite additive quotient of the microscopic integer crossing count.

## Observer-equivalent current law

Across all additive local observers, the same structural law survives:

`local reversal -> equal-and-opposite transfer -> conserved global sum`.

The numerical labels can differ between observers, but the current/conservation structure is invariant.

## What is and is not observer-independent

**Robust:**

- locality of the transfer;
- equal-and-opposite update;
- conservation of the total additive label;
- cyclic structure of any finite image;
- conjugation by orientation reversal when the observer respects sign inversion.

**Not uniquely fixed:**

- the modulus `m`;
- the normalization of the local unit;
- nonlinear/nonadditive coarse-grainings;
- the interpretation of the conserved label as electric charge.

A nonlinear observer map can destroy the clean additive current law. Therefore the result is conditional on the observer preserving local composition/additivity.

## Interpretation

The important result is not quotient uniqueness but **structural observer robustness**:

`integer microscopic reversal current`

maps under every additive local observer to

`cyclic observer current with conserved total`.

Finite observers necessarily see a cyclic quotient `Z_m`.

This strengthens the Wheeler chain because the `Z_m` residue description need not be inserted as a special-purpose encoding; it is the universal finite additive image of the underlying integer boundary current.

However, WHEELER-12 does not by itself select `m=3`. Threefold selection still requires the additional criteria developed in WHEELER-08 through WHEELER-10.

## Strongest combined statement so far

Within the current toy framework:

1. elementary reversal generates an integer local divergence/current;
2. additive local observers preserve the current law;
3. finite observer images are cyclic `Z_m` quotients;
4. requiring neutral plus one distinct conjugate pair makes `m=3` the minimal admissible finite quotient;
5. simple monotone repair costs and connected unit-transfer dynamics also favor `m=3` within that model family.

This remains a mathematical toy mechanism, not a derivation of Standard Model electric charge.
