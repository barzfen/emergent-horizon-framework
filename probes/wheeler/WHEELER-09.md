# WHEELER-09 — Minimal admissible cyclic sector / dynamical-selection audit

**Status: PARTIAL PASS — m=3 is selected within this toy family by a combination of structural admissibility and dynamical economy, but the result is model-dependent and is not a derivation of physical electric charge.**

## Question

Given the generic `Z_m` fractionalization mechanism of WHEELER-07 and the reversal/conjugation structure of WHEELER-08, can the same local dynamics discriminate among `m = 2,3,4,...` without explicitly rewarding `m=3`?

The anti-tuning rule is that the dynamical law must be identical for every `m`.

## Structural admissibility

Under reversal `r -> -r (mod m)`, require at least:

- one neutral class `0`;
- one nonzero class with a **distinct** conjugate partner.

Then:

- `m=2` fails: its only nonzero residue is self-conjugate (`1 = -1 mod 2`);
- `m=3` is the smallest admissible case: `{0}` plus one conjugate pair `{1,2}`;
- `m>=4` introduces extra conjugacy sectors and/or extra nonzero self-conjugate structure.

Thus `m=3` is the minimal cyclic structure supporting a neutral sector and one genuine `+/-` pair.

## Local repair-cost audit

Use a local unit correction rule: a defect of residue `r` may be repaired by unit steps `+1` or `-1` on the cyclic residue space. The shortest repair cost is

`c_m(r) = min(r, m-r)`.

For uniformly distributed defects, the mean repair cost is

`C(m) = (1/m) sum_{r=0}^{m-1} min(r,m-r)`.

Numerical values:

- `m=2`: `0.5` (structurally inadmissible)
- `m=3`: `2/3 ~= 0.6667`
- `m=4`: `1`
- `m=5`: `1.2`
- `m=6`: `1.5`
- `m=7`: `12/7 ~= 1.7143`
- `m=8`: `2`

Once `m=2` is excluded by the conjugacy requirement, `m=3` has the lowest repair cost and `C(m)` increases with `m`.

This is an economy result, not a unique physical principle: the smallest admissible residue space is also cheapest to repair under this chosen local metric.

## Common nearest-neighbour dynamics

Consider a cyclic chain of `n` local residues `x_i in Z_m` subject to global closure

`sum_i x_i = 0 mod m`.

Apply the same nearest-neighbour transfer move for every `m`:

`(x_i, x_{i+1}) -> (x_i + 1, x_{i+1} - 1) mod m`

and its inverse. This preserves the global residue exactly and does not reference `m=3` specially.

The state space has `m^(n-1)` closed configurations. For the unweighted continuous-time walk generated by these local transfers, finite exact diagonalization for `n=4,5` and `m=2,...,7` gives an `n`-independent lowest nonzero gap in these tested cases:

- `m=2`: `4` (but structurally inadmissible)
- `m=3`: `6`
- `m=4`: `4`
- `m=5`: `2.763932...`
- `m=6`: `2`
- `m=7`: `1.506041...`

These values match

`lambda_1(m) = 8 sin^2(pi/m)`

for `m>=3` in this model.

Therefore among the structurally admissible cases `m>=3`, the spectral gap is maximal at

`m=3`,

and decreases monotonically as `m` increases. In the Markov interpretation, `m=3` is the fastest-mixing member of this family.

## Combined result

Within this specific cyclic toy family, three independent observations line up:

1. **Minimality:** `m=3` is the smallest cyclic structure that supports neutral plus a distinct conjugate pair.
2. **Repair economy:** among structurally admissible `m`, `m=3` minimizes average unit repair cost.
3. **Dynamical relaxation:** under the same local nearest-neighbour transfer rule, `m=3` maximizes the spectral gap among admissible `m>=3`.

So the model produces

`structural requirement + local economy + common dynamics -> preference for m=3`.

## Caution

This is not yet an explanation of quark charge thirds.

The preference depends on the selected family (`Z_m` residues), local repair metric, and nearest-neighbour transfer dynamics. A different equally natural model could rank the sectors differently. Therefore the strongest defensible statement is:

> In one untuned and internally consistent EHF-inspired cyclic family, the first structure capable of supporting a distinct conjugate pair (`m=3`) is also the cheapest to repair and the fastest to relax under a common local dynamics.

The next meaningful test should be robustness: vary the local move set, boundary/growth rule, and local cost function without introducing explicit `m` dependence and ask how often `m=3` remains selected. If the preference disappears under small modelling changes, WHEELER-09 is a toy-model artifact. If it survives a broad class of admissible local rules, it becomes substantially more interesting.
