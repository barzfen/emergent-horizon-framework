# 1. Why an atlas now

The GROW branch has accumulated enough exact structure that another isolated theorem would be less useful than a comparative view.

The key question is now:

> **What exactly distinguishes \(LR2,LR3,LR4,\ldots\), and which distinctions could plausibly matter in a later physics bridge?**

The atlas therefore compares channels using only already-certified invariants.

No new physical identification is assumed.

---

# 2. Common property of every channel

Every effective reversal-support relation lies in the same nearest raw shell:

\[
\boxed{
d_E=2.
}
\]

Thus:

\[
LR2,\ LR3,\ LR4,\ldots
\]

all have the same raw HC displacement.

This is a major constraint on interpretation.

A future physical distinction among support channels cannot come merely from:

- raw edge distance;
- generic raw class abundance;
- membership in the nearest shell.

The discriminants lie in their **internal support structure and growth behavior**.

\[

# 18. Criteria for a legitimate physics bridge

A future bridge should not begin by naming channels after known particles.

Instead, choose a known physical structure and ask whether its independently defined invariants match the HC channel atlas.

A convincing bridge would need several simultaneous matches, for example:

1. a discrete channel index;
2. an alternating binary property;
3. nearest-neighbor channel coupling;
4. threshold structure;
5. matching multiplicity laws;
6. matching scaling exponents;
7. matching sector action;
8. a principled operational relation between growth scale and physical observable.

One accidental numerical resemblance is not enough.

# 21. Recommended next move

The atlas changes the research question.

Rather than proving another generic support identity immediately, the next useful task is to choose one of two directions:

## A. Structural bridge test

Search known mathematical physics for independently defined coupled-channel structures with:

- alternating \(\mathbb Z_2\) character;
- nearest-neighbor channel transitions;

Then compare invariants without fitting names.

The second route is safer if the priority remains deriving more structure before looking outward.
