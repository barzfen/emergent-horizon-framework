# EHF-XBR-01
## OPW Universal-Core / AMP Middle-Block Growth Identification

### Purpose

Establish whether the OPW3 universal descriptors

\[
(3,3),\qquad(3,4)
\]

are exactly the amplitude branch's documented middle-block insertion channel.

The two branches use different label conventions:

- OPW3: \(0,\ldots,n-1\);
- AMP: \(1,\ldots,n\).

At \(7\to9\), the OPW newest labels \(7,8\) correspond to AMP legs \(8,9\).

### State-level identification

In the OPW sequential-gap convention:

- descriptor \((3,3)\) inserts the newest labels at positions \(4,5\) in
  order \(8,7\);
- descriptor \((3,4)\) inserts them at positions \(4,5\) in order \(7,8\).

After adding one to every label, this is precisely the AMP condition

\[
\{c[4],c[5]\}=\{8,9\}.
\]

The probe compares the complete set of source-state/child-state incidences
generated by both definitions.

### Distinguish the anchor channel

The amplitude-side GROW-01 anchor composite is a different map:

- insert each newest label immediately right or left of the anchor;
- canonicalize after each insertion.

At chamber level the documented reconciliation is

\[
D(P)=\{A(P),\operatorname{smaller}(P)\},
\]

\[
G(P)=\{A(P),A(\operatorname{flip}P)\}.
\]

The channels share exactly the \(+144\) child \(A(P)\).

Once the state-growth maps are identified, map-level theorems can be shared
between branches. Functor-specific conclusions do not transfer automatically:

- amplitude relation ranks and sibling-equality mechanisms remain amplitude
  results;
- OPW packet closures and spectral projectors remain dynamics results.
