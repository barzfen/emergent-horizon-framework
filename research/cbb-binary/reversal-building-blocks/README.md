# Can one kind of reversal generate the symmetry?

**Date** 18 November 2026, Australia/Brisbane  
**Status** Exact constructions and obstruction, with a finite exhaustive check.  
**Scope** $SO(3)$, $U(1)$, $U(n)$ and $SU(n)$, followed by a fixed-window cycle experiment. Admissible comparison is the chosen primitive data of EHF.

## One operation type, with different placements

A real hyperplane reflection flips the component of a vector perpendicular to the hyperplane and fixes every component in it. Applying the same reflection twice gives the identity. Applying reflections about two different lines in a plane rotates that plane through twice the signed angle between the lines. In three dimensions, take two mirror planes that meet along the desired axis. Their intersection with a perpendicular cross-section gives the two reflection lines. More generally, Cartan–Dieudonné gives a factorization of every real orthogonal map into hyperplane reflections. An orientation-preserving map uses an even number of them. The construction uses a real inner product and allows the reflection direction to vary. [Eckhard Meinrenken, *Clifford Algebras and Lie Groups*, §4, lecture notes](https://www.math.toronto.edu/mein/teaching/LieClifford/cl12.pdf).

| Group | Construction | Data used to identify the group |
| --- | --- | --- |
| $U(1)\cong SO(2)$ | Two reflections in a real plane give one phase rotation. | A Euclidean plane, with an orientation if the sign of phase is to be named. |
| $SO(3)$ | Two reflections in planes give a spatial rotation. | A three-dimensional real inner product and the orientation-preserving condition. |
| $U(n)$ | Use real reflections in $2n$ dimensions. Keep the resulting orthogonal maps that commute with a common quarter-turn $J$. | A real inner product and an orthogonal complex structure $J$, satisfying $J^2=-I$. |
| $SU(n)$ | Within $U(n)$, keep the maps of complex determinant one. Equivalently, take the subgroup generated by commutators. | The same unitary structure. Determinant one can be recovered from the composition law of $U(n)$. |

For the unitary line of the table, identify $\mathbb C^n$ with $\mathbb R^{2n}$. Multiplication by $i$ becomes $J$. Commuting with $J$ is precisely complex linearity, and an orthogonal complex-linear map preserves the Hermitian inner product. Every such map has an orthonormal complex eigenbasis and acts on each corresponding real plane by a rotation. Factoring each plane rotation gives a construction with at most $2n$ real reflections. Individual factors may fail to preserve $J$. This is a construction in a larger real space. [Robert L. Bryant, *Manifolds with Special Holonomy*, lecture 2, p. 1 (2003)](https://legacy.slmath.org/workshops/247/schedules/1551/documents/3505/assets/33116) · [Richard Kueng, *Quantum Computing*, Corollary 11.3](https://www.jku.at/fileadmin/gruppen/180/2024_kueng_quantum_computing.pdf).

For the last line, determinants of commutators are one. Conversely, any determinant-one diagonal unitary is a product of balanced pair phases $\operatorname{diag}(e^{it},e^{-it})$ on pairs of coordinates. Each balanced pair is $DPD^{-1}P^{-1}$, where $D=\operatorname{diag}(e^{it},1)$ and $P$ swaps the two coordinates. Diagonalize in a unitary basis and conjugate the factors back. This proves $SU(n)=[U(n),U(n)]$, with brackets denoting the subgroup generated by commutators.

The audit counts one **type** of operation. One fixed reflection has order two. Reflections with varied directions carry placement information, and the unitary construction uses the same $J$ across its planes. The factorization by itself leaves the inner product, the dimension and the choice of compatible $J$ as inputs.

There is an exact obstruction to selecting that $J$ from rotation symmetry alone in real dimension $2n\ge4$. Any canonical choice would commute with every real rotation. For any nonzero vector $v$, all rotations fixing $v$ would then also fix $Jv$. Those rotations have only the line through $v$ as their common fixed space, so $Jv$ would have to be parallel to $v$. That is impossible for a real map squaring to $-I$. A relation that selects a common quarter-turn supplies information beyond the full real rotation symmetry.

## Fixed-window reversal on a cycle

Take a marked consecutive block of $m\ge3$ individually labelled vertices with a fixed left flank and right flank. Keep the exterior fixed. Reverse the first $m-1$ slots, then reverse all $m$ slots. The composite sends $x_1\ldots x_m$ to $x_mx_1\ldots x_{m-1}$. With distinct labels this gives an action of the cyclic group $C_m$ with exact period $m$. Either reversal alone has order two. The marked window fixes the comparison used to read shifts.

There is a conditional route from these finite shifts to a circle. Choose nested levels with $m=2^k$, identify each coarse shift with two finer shifts, and measure shortest shift distance as a fraction of the total period. These identifications preserve that metric. The union is the dyadic fractions modulo one, dense in the circle, so its metric completion is $U(1)$. The refinement, its identifications and the normalization are part of this construction. Each finite level gives a finite cyclic group.

### Exactly when the two moves survive the three-colour rule

Use the proper-colouring constraint from the [three-colour growth series](../GROWTH-CLOCKS-AND-BELL.md): two adjacent vertices must differ, and colours travel with labels. For this experiment allow the two tested lengths and require a proper cycle after each individual reversal. Test an indefinitely repeatable schedule with fixed flanks and a fixed block. This includes any reversal that exchanges labels while preserving the visible colour word, alongside any colour-changing reversal.

**Proposition.** A full orbit is legal exactly when the flanks have the same colour and an even-length block alternates between the other two colours. The labelled period is $m$ and the colour-only period, sampled after each two-move composite, is two.

**Proof.** A full orbit puts every block vertex next to each flank. Thus all used block colours must differ from both flank colours. Distinct flank colours would leave one possible colour for the whole block, which violates adjacent inequality. Equal flanks leave two usable colours. Successive shifts expose every cyclic adjacency of the block, including its original last–first pair. Hence the block must alternate cyclically and its length must be even. Conversely, on such a block the first reversal has odd length and preserves its colour word. The second reversal reverses an even alternating word, producing the other alternating colour word. Both steps preserve proper colouring, including the flank edges. Iteration flips the visible colour word each round and shifts the distinct labels by one slot. ∎

For example, take the block `ABAB` between two `C` flanks. After two composites the colours look as they did initially. The labelled block is halfway through its four-shift orbit. An exterior vertex of colour `A` can connect the two `C` flanks to complete the full cycle, so the example embeds in an actual proper cycle. In general, a vertex with a colour different from both flanks suffices to complete each proper local test word without modifying the move region.

There is also a locality obstruction inside each surviving block. In the raw cycle, adjacent swaps can build either reversal. In a surviving alternating block of length at least four, every adjacent swap inside the block creates an equal-colour neighbour pair, since the swapped pair has at least one further block neighbour. Thus an adjacent-swap decomposition preserving the same window and testing proper colouring at every intermediate step cannot even start. This statement allows routes using other windows or other intermediate objects to be studied separately.

### Verification

Run `python3 research/cbb-binary/reversal-building-blocks/check_reversal_building_blocks.py` from the repository root. The standard-library verifier checks every raw shift and return for block lengths 3–256. It simulates both intermediate reversals on all 12,240 proper three-colour local words with block lengths 3–10. Exactly 24 remain legal throughout the full sweep, six at each of lengths 4, 6, 8 and 10. Every survivor has labelled period $m$ and colour-only period two, as the proof predicts.

The obstruction applies to this fixed-window protocol. Its use of arbitrary reversal lengths and explicitly retained label moves differs from a colour-changing LR3-only pulse. Other windows, schedules and observables can carry more information. Earlier [CROSS-58](../CROSS-58-growth-reference.md) tracks a different ambiguity in a supplied coherent model. Here the transport is a permutation of labels. The open EHF reconstruction is a phase-sensitive comparison and compatible noncommuting transports on the same observable space. The group factorizations do not establish that physical symmetries use these generators.
