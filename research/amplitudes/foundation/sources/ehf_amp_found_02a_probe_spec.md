# EHF-AMP-FOUND-02A
## Growth–Factorisation Compatibility

**Status:** executable probe specification  
**Layer:** move-independent Hamilton-cycle ordering / Parke–Taylor relation layer  
**Foundational move dependency:** none; LR3, OPW3 and LR23 do not enter this computation

## 1. Question

The exact combinatorial growth map inserts the new maximum label next to the anchor,
while the matching collinear residue deletes that label. At the level of individual
Hamilton-cycle orderings these maps are exact inverse sections.

The probe asks whether this inverse relationship survives at the level of amplitude
relations:

\[
\text{growth insertion}\quad\leftrightarrow\quad\text{factorisation deletion}.
\]

The central distinction is between an exact state map and an induced map on relation
spaces. The former does not automatically imply the latter.

## 2. Fixed spaces

Let \(X_n\) be the reflection-reduced canonical Hamilton-cycle ordering space and let
\(\Pi\) be the anchor-1 parity-position partition. For a fibre \(F\subset X_n\), let

\[
R_n(F)\subset \mathbb R^F
\]

be its constant-coefficient Parke–Taylor relation space.

For \(m=n+1\), define the two anchor-side insertion maps

\[
i_n^+,i_n^-:X_n\to X_m,
\]

by inserting label \(m\) immediately to the right or left of anchor 1 and then
canonicalising. The oriented coefficient maps \(I_n^\pm\) include the sign induced by
canonical orientation, so the corresponding residue satisfies

\[
D_m I_n^\pm=\operatorname{id}
\]

on the ambient coefficient space.

This state-level identity is exact by construction. Whether

\[
I_n^\pm R_n(F)\subseteq R_m(G)
\]

is a separate numerical and structural question.

## 3. Four non-equivalent tests

### Q0. State-level section

For every cycle and both anchor-side branches, verify

\[
D_m I_n^\pm(c)=c.
\]

This is the combinatorial baseline.

### Q1. Unchanged-coefficient relation lift

For each parent fibre \(F\) and branch \(\pm\), insert each relation vector with the
same coefficients, including only the orientation sign required by canonicalisation.
Test:

- fresh Parke–Taylor residual at multiplicity \(m\);
- containment residual in the child relation space \(R_m(G_\pm)\);
- exact intersection dimension between the insertion span and \(R_m(G_\pm)\).

A failure means the state section does **not** induce a relation section by unchanged
coefficients. It does not rule out a nontrivial lift.

### Q2. Factorisation inheritance

For every child fibre \(G\), restrict the physical max-label residue to its genuine
relation space:

\[
D_m\vert_{R_m(G)}:R_m(G)\to R_n(F).
\]

Aggregate all child images arriving in a parent fibre \(F\) and measure

\[
\operatorname{rank}\left(
\sum_{G\to F}D_m R_m(G)
\right)
\]

against \(\dim R_n(F)\).

If equality holds, every parent relation is factorisation-inherited through the
new-label channel. If not, the parent has relations not generated by that channel.

### Q3. Branch versus physical residue

Split the residue into right- and left-anchor contributions and test each separately,
as well as their signed physical combination. This determines whether relationhood
requires cancellation between the two branches.

### Q4. Channel-new child directions

For each child fibre, compute

\[
\dim\ker\left(D_m\vert_{R_m(G)}\right).
\]

These are relations invisible to this particular residue channel. They are called
**channel-new** or **residue-invisible**, not absolutely new: another chart, channel,
or nontrivial lift may still generate them.

The probe also computes the exact intersection

\[
R_m(G)\cap
\operatorname{span}\bigl(I_n^+R_n, I_n^-R_n\bigr),
\]

which measures relations inherited by the simplest unchanged-coefficient insertion.

## 4. Exceptional n=8 direction

The AMP-18 audit found one relation direction in

\[
E=\{1,4,5,6\},\qquad O=\{2,3,7,8\}
\]

that is absent from the complete anchor-1 \(9\to8\) residue image.

EHF-AMP-FOUND-02A reconstructs that unique direction and asks:

1. Is it annihilated by the \(8\to7\) max-label residue?
2. Does it lie in the exact relation-space intersection generated by anchor-side
   insertion from \(n=7\)?
3. Is it outside unchanged-coefficient inheritance but inside the residue kernel?

Only the third pattern supports the guarded description **channel-new candidate at
7→8**. This is not a claim of absolute birth or physical particle creation.

## 5. Default transitions

\[
6\to7,\qquad7\to8,\qquad8\to9.
\]

These include both parity types observed in the combinatorial growth audit:

- even→odd: the two anchor branches land in the same child fibre;
- odd→even: the branches land in distinct child fibres.

## 6. Numerical safeguards

- fresh independent Parke–Taylor validation for bases and residue images;
- rank-truncated SVD with relative and absolute thresholds;
- principal-angle intersection tests rather than projection rank alone;
- no wide-matrix QR membership test;
- explicit distinction between branch-only and signed physical residue maps;
- machine-readable CSV and JSON output.

## 8. Verdict language

Permitted conclusions include:

- **state section only:** insertion/deletion are inverse on orderings but not on local
  relation spaces;
- **factorisation inheritance:** the max-label residue is surjective onto selected or
  all parent local relation spaces;
- **branch-coupled relationhood:** only the signed combination of left/right branches
  is a relation;
- **channel-new directions:** a quantified residue kernel exists;
- **exceptional direction channel-new candidate:** the AMP-18 missing vector is in the
  \(8\to7\) residue kernel and outside the unchanged-coefficient inherited relation
  intersection.

The probe does not derive gauge theory, particle content, spacetime, or a physical
growth law.
