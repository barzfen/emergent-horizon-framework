# Soft angular contrast integrated over outgoing shapes

This extends the [weighted beam-orientation test](weighted-angles.md). It uses the same full-colour six-gluon helicity sum and leading-soft emission current. It varies the outgoing shape at fixed collision energy as well as the beam direction.

Treat the outgoing pair as a cluster P with mass m and set the collision energy Q to one. Use rho = m/Q as the expansion parameter. For each rho, integrate x = 2 E_P/Q from 0.46 to 0.54 and y = 2 E_4/Q from 0.625 to 17/24. These energy coordinates span a rectangular patch centred at the earlier (3,4,5) outgoing energy proportions. The third energy follows from conservation, and the two recoil directions follow from the momentum triangle. The pair daughters have equal energies and their opening plane coincides with the recoil plane. In the beam orientation integral, the polar angle from the parent axis is 65–115 degrees and the beam azimuth from the opening plane is 45–90 degrees. Soft directions are integrated over the 20–40 degree annulus around the parent.

At fixed Q and m, the three-body phase-space measure for the cluster and two spectators is flat in the two Dalitz variables, or equivalently the two energy coordinates used here. The pair decay coordinates are held fixed. Their common phase-space factors and the common flux cancel in the normalized moments. See the [PDG kinematics review](https://pdg.lbl.gov/2025/reviews/rpp2025-rev-kinematics.pdf), three-body decay phase space. The integration uses the hard colour- and helicity-summed tree matrix element squared as its weight and recalculates it at each rho.

The reported normalized angular contrast is the integrated radiation moment with weight cos(2 phi), divided by the integrated radiation intensity over the same accepted region. Phi is the soft azimuth measured from the opening plane. The coefficient of rho² measures the change from the zero-opening contrast. It uses a different opening parameter and recoil path from the previous fixed-shape study.

Run `OPENBLAS_NUM_THREADS=2 python3 shape_average.py` with NumPy. The script prints each grid result before the final JSON. The saved results report the three quadrature grids and minimum sampled soft-to-hard angular separations. The script asserts agreement between opening extrapolations. To reproduce the centre control, run the same command with `--centre`. These are restricted partonic phase-space averages, with the equal-energy pair and opening orientation held fixed. The calculation does not include a soft-energy integral, parton densities, or a jet definition.

The finest grid gives a normalized contrast coefficient of +50.8959, compared with +50.6088 at the centre shape. The two finest grids differ by about 0.04%. See the [integration output](shape-average-results.json) and the [centre control](shape-average-centre.json).
