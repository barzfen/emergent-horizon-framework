# What selects the nine-point cancellation symmetries?

This audit follows Barry Fensom’s [nine-point tail](../README.md). It distinguishes what follows from the two-sided chamber pairing and what follows from the support-restricted Kleiss–Kuijf shuffle constraints.

## The local pairing treats all four symmetry sectors equally

Work with anchor 1 and deleted label 9 in the reference A = (4,5,8), B = (2,3,6,7). There are 144 active words ending in 9. After deleting 9, summation identifies the two orientations of each of 72 target words. The kernel K of this map has one basis direction per target orientation pair, the difference of the two preimages.

Let H be the order-four group generated by swapping (4 5) and (6 7). Its action on the 72 unoriented target words is free. A nonidentity element cannot fix an oriented word because it changes a label. It also cannot take an anchored word to its reversal: reversal fixes only the antipodal position among the seven non-anchor positions, whereas every element of H fixes at least the three non-anchor labels 2, 3 and 8. Each orbit of four target pairs therefore gives a copy of the regular representation on the associated four-dimensional difference space, even if the chosen signs of the pair differences vary. Thus K has dimension 18 in each character sector.

This count uses just the chamber pairing and label symmetries. The selection of the two exceptional lines needs the lift constraints.

## Derivation from the inactive shuffle columns

For a cut d, let S_d be the span of the KK shuffle columns of the allowed words whose collision residue is zero. Use KK coordinates with endpoints 1 and 9. The active words are distinct basis columns spanning E. To be the active coefficient vector of a relation, a vector in E must be cancellable by inactive columns. The allowed active image is therefore S_d ∩ E.

Extend the orientation summation to all KK coordinates by leaving every coordinate outside E separate. This extended map has kernel K inside E. Its restriction to S_d has kernel S_d ∩ K, exactly the cancellation space. Hence, in each character sector, one can get the active rank by subtracting the rank of the exterior projection from the rank of the inactive shuffle matrix. The cancellation rank is obtained by subtracting the rank after orientation summation instead. The script constructs signed orbit sums of inactive words and computes these ranks directly, without first constructing source relation bases or using saved images.

The rank-8 and rank-9 cuts have different allowed additions to the common cut. For their union the inactive spans add, but taking their intersections with E need not commute with addition. Likewise, summation can create a shared image that is larger than the image of their intersection.

| Character under (4 5), (6 7) | Local pairing kernel | Sum of separate branch cancellations | Cancellations in the sum of active images | Cancellations in the union cut |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| ++ | 18 | 7 | 7 | 7 |
| +− | 18 | 8 | 9 | 9 |
| −+ | 18 | 6 | 6 | 6 |
| −− | 18 | 10 | 10 | 11 |

The +− sector has no common active direction. The two branch active images have ranks 3 and 10 there. Their folded images have ranks 1 and 4, while their folded sum has rank 4. The one folded direction of the rank-8 branch is therefore shared with the rank-9 branch in this sector. This is the extra shared residue.

In the −− sector the separate active images have ranks 6 and 10 and intersect in one dimension. Their sum has rank 15, while the union cut has active rank 16. Both have folded rank 5. The extra active direction from the union can therefore be represented by a vector in K, giving the second cancellation.

## Scope and reproduction

Run `PYTHONPATH=.. python3 shuffle_sectors.py > shuffle-sectors-results.json` with standard-library Python. The output records all character ranks of the inactive KK span, its projection off the active coordinates, and its orientation-summed projection. The local equality of the four multiplicities has an orbit proof. The unequal lift-constrained counts have an exact shuffle-matrix derivation. A geometric rule predicting the latter counts remains open.
