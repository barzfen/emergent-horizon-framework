# EHF-HC-06 · Benchmarking boundary cuts and compact matching rows

**COMPUTATIONAL.** Choosing splits for smaller boundaries reduced solve time on nine of ten workloads and the estimated cost including planning on five. The largest gain in this suite was the 20-vertex grid, from about 146 ms to 6 ms including planning. On $K_{4,4}$ the smaller maximum boundary increased the number of attempted joins and slowed the solve.

**EXACT.** Using the established matching basis as the column set preserves the row dependencies and therefore the representatives chosen by [HC-05](HC-05.md#compress-a-family-by-its-completion-answers). The verifier reconstructed every full matrix entry through eight open ends. On a fixed sample of 64 rows with ten open ends, the compact row construction took about 3 ms instead of 184 ms.

## Decomposition and comparison

The two plans feed the same exact solver. The **sorted** plan recursively divides the lexicographically sorted edge list in half. The **small-boundary** plan also considers edge orders obtained from breadth-first distances within the current piece, starting at each of its vertices. It tries splits whose children each take between one third and two thirds of the edges. It first minimises the larger child boundary, then the union of the child boundaries, then their total size, then the difference in child edge counts. The boundary includes the relevant fixed terminals. Candidate cuts use graph incidence and the two terminal labels.

Each plan runs with all attainable records and with the compact basis reduction. Where the largest boundary has at most ten vertices, the verifier also runs the full-column reduction as a control. Repeated runs of every configuration agreed with an independent, memoised whole-graph path search. The full and compact reductions produced the same structural counters in every case where both ran.

The suite has five feasible and five infeasible queries. The grids are rectangular nearest-neighbour graphs. The relabelled cases use a reproducible permutation with seed 196. In particular, the two positive 15-vertex grid cases differ only by relabelling. The denser cases are complete graphs or complete bipartite graphs. This is a targeted suite for comparing decompositions of one exact algorithm.

## Compact matching columns

For one boundary degree vector, a row has one coordinate per exterior matching. Its coordinate is one when the interior and exterior matchings join into a single cycle after formal terminal closure. The explicit basis matchings $X_t$ of Cygan, Kratsch and Nederlof form a column basis over $\mathbb F_2$. If a linear combination of attainable rows vanishes on these columns, it vanishes on every full column because each full column is a linear combination of the basis columns. The converse follows by restriction. Thus processing attainable rows in the same order and retaining a row when it extends the span keeps the same representatives with either coordinate set. The verifier also uses the complement-bit formula of their factorisation to reconstruct the complete matrices for $t=2,4,6,8$. [Cygan, Kratsch and Nederlof (2018), Definition 3.1 and Theorem 3.4](https://doi.org/10.1145/3148227)

For a boundary of size $b$, ignoring tighter restrictions at the fixed terminals, choose an even number $t$ of degree-one vertices and give the other $b-t$ vertices degrees zero or two. The established rank gives at most $2^{t/2-1}$ retained records for that degree profile when $t>0$, and there is one empty matching when $t=0$. Summing the profile bounds gives

$$
2^{b-1}+\frac{(2+\sqrt{2})^b+(2-\sqrt{2})^b}{4}.
$$

That is an upper bound on retained table size. Graph incidence, endpoint requirements and the decomposition decide how many of its states actually occur.

### Row construction and whole-solver effects

The row-only test builds 64 distinct, deterministically sampled rows with empty caches. Both coordinate sets selected the same independent rows for the sample. These measurements include constructing the column sets and testing every sampled row against them.

| Open ends | Full columns | Basis columns | Full build ms | Compact build ms |
| ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 8 | 105 | 8 | 17.182 | 1.399 |
| 10 | 945 | 16 | 184.168 | 2.957 |

In the 20- and 24-vertex grids, every attained row was retained even when reduction ran. The reduction chiefly helped on the denser cases in this suite. On the chosen $K_9$ plan it discarded 226 rows across the reduced run, lowered peak retained rows from 481 to 330, and lowered join pairs from 169,084 to 98,720. The solve median was 148.742 ms for all records, 101.120 ms for compact reduction, and 91.565 ms for full-column reduction in this run. Both reductions reached the same tables and join counts. The row-only speedup therefore has a different scope from the whole-solver timings.

## Reproduce

Run with Python 3.12 and its standard library from the repository root. The [verifier](verify_hc_06.py) builds all inputs and prints the rank audit, timings and exact operation counts. Each configuration is verified against the oracle. The [HC-05 verifier](verify_hc_05.py) also passed the previous exhaustive comparisons after the changes to the shared solver.

```sh
python3 research/hamiltonian-cycle/verify_hc_06.py --repeats 9
```
