# EHF-HC-05 · Boundary pairings across successive joins

**PROVED.** A partial path packing can be represented exactly by its boundary degrees and the complete matching of its open ends. This record composes across any binary grouping of edge pieces. For the yes-or-no completion question, a basis of attainable matching rows can be retained after every join without changing the answer.

**NEGATIVE.** At six open ends, recording which pairs occur individually can accept a pairing that no path packing realises. The smallest processed piece giving a false completion answer this way has eight vertices. Minimality for the seven-vertex case is computational.

**COMPUTATIONAL.** Eight open ends give 105 distinct completion rows of binary rank eight. For the complete family of all 105 matchings, five attainable rows cover all exterior completions and four cannot. The graph audit agreed with a direct spanning-path oracle in 7,732 checks through order six.

## What passes through a boundary

Return to the fixed-entry, fixed-exit spanning path in [HC-04](HC-04.md#exact-composition-rule). Let $x,y$ be distinct global terminals. They require degree one in the selected path, and every other vertex requires degree two. Split the edges of the graph into any binary tree of pieces. The vertex scope of a piece is the set of endpoints of its available edges. Its boundary consists of scope vertices incident to an edge outside the piece, together with $x,y$ whenever they occur in scope. A terminal stays on the boundary even after all its incident edges have been processed.

An admissible partial selection from a piece is an acyclic union of paths. Every vertex outside its boundary in the scope has already reached degree two. Boundary vertices record their selected degree, from zero up to the global requirement. Every nontrivial path has two ends on the boundary. Its boundary record consists of the degree vector and the matching of the degree-one vertices into pairs belonging to the same path. This matching is a whole simultaneous choice. A degree-zero boundary vertex still needs incident edges, while a degree-two boundary vertex has already been used inside a path.

### Join rule and proof

1. Add child degrees at shared boundary vertices. Reject a degree above its global requirement. A vertex leaving the boundary must have reached degree two.
2. If a vertex has degree one in both children, concatenate their paths at that vertex. Reject a closed cycle formed in the process, including a loop made by two equal endpoint pairs carried by different child paths. Retain the surviving ends as the new matching.

Each child edge set is disjoint from the other. Their selected paths can meet only at a shared boundary vertex. A meeting that obeys the degree bound either concatenates two path ends or leaves the selected edges on a single side. The matching records exactly which ends already lie on a common path, so it detects every closure. Any vertex already outside the boundary of its child remains outside the join boundary and has no new incident edges. Conversely, splitting any admissible joined selection at the child edge sets gives two admissible selections with these records. Thus enumerating child records and applying the rule is exact. At the full edge set, the required record is degrees $1,1$ and the single pair $\{x,y\}$. No other component can survive because cycles were rejected and all other vertices have degree two.

The rule depends only on each piece and its external incidence. In particular, equal records within a piece have the same answers against every fixed exterior edge selection. Repeated joins can group the same edges in any order. This is the usual connectivity state underlying Hamiltonian dynamic programming. This probe expresses it as an admissible-comparison relation and applies it to the completion problem developed in HC-01 through HC-04. [Cygan, Kratsch and Nederlof (2018), §4](https://doi.org/10.1145/3148227)

## What cannot be merged

**Degrees alone.** Use terminals $0,3$, with boundary vertices $0,1,2,3$ and an interior vertex $4$. One selection consists of paths $(0,1)$ and $(2,4,3)$. Another consists of $(0,3)$ and $(1,4,2)$. Both have boundary degrees $1,1,1,1$. The exterior edge $12$ makes the first selection a spanning path and gives the second a separate cycle. Four open ends are the first size with different matchings on the same ends.

**Pairing alone.** In a processed triangle on $0,1,2$, the selections $(0,1)$ and $(0,2,1)$ induce the same pair $(0,1)$. Their degrees at $2$ are zero and two respectively. An exterior path $(1,3,2,4)$ completes the first selection with terminals $0,4$. The second selection overloads vertex $2$.

### The individual pairs do not determine a family of matchings

In a piece with open ends $0$ through $5$ and interior vertices $6,7$, use edges $01,03,05,16,23,26,34,35,36,47,57,67$. All open ends have required piece degree one, and both interior vertices have degree two. The five attainable matchings are shown below. The verifier obtains them by joining edge pieces.

| Attainable matching | Pair it shares with the exterior matching $01,23,45$ |
| --- | --- |
| $01,23,45$ | All three |
| $01,24,35$ | $01$ |
| $01,25,34$ | $01$ |
| $03,12,45$ | $45$ |
| $05,14,23$ | $23$ |

The exterior matching $01,23,45$ can be realised for an open spanning path by connecting $0,1$ to the two global terminals and realising $23$ and $45$ through separate new degree-two vertices. Those exterior edges are forced. Each real interior matching shares a pair with the exterior, so the result has a separate closed component after closing the global terminals. The pair union also contains $03,14,25$. These three pairs come from different attainable matchings, and their hypothetical combination connects with the exterior into one cycle. The corresponding full 12-vertex graph has no spanning path with the specified terminals, as checked by direct enumeration.

For at most four open ends every pair determines its whole matching on the end set. With six ends and no other used vertex, the attainable matchings are just the perfect matchings in the available graph on the ends, so the pair union reconstructs them exactly. At seven vertices there is at most one other used vertex. A degree-two vertex must occur between the ends of one pair, with the other pairs realised by direct edges. The verifier tried this condition at every choice of that vertex in all 853 connected graph shapes on seven vertices and checked every exterior matching. It found no false acceptance. If the processed graph is disconnected and has an admissible packing, the end set splits into even groups across components. In a split using all six ends, each group has at most four ends, which are reconstructed exactly and can be combined independently. An isolated component requiring degree two and having no path ends makes the table empty. Boundary vertices of degree zero can be removed from this test. Together these cases establish the eight-vertex minimum for a false completion answer from pair marginals.

## Compress a family by its completion answers

Fix the boundary degree vector and write $T$ for its degree-one vertices. The degrees already determine whether a proposed exterior can meet all global degree requirements. For a compatible exterior, add a formal closing edge between $x,y$. Viewed on the exterior side of the cut, its paths then induce another perfect matching $N$ on $T$. If that exterior already contains a cycle and $T$ is nonempty, it cannot complete the interior into one cycle. Otherwise an inside matching $M$ succeeds exactly when the multigraph $M\cup N$ is connected. The extra edge is formal, so two parallel matching edges on two ends count as one connected cycle. The empty-end case has at most one state per degree vector and needs no reduction.

Distinct single matchings have different completion rows. For $M\ne M'$, take an edge $e\in M'\setminus M$. Its ends belong to different pairs of $M$. Order the pairs of $M$ around an alternating cycle so that $e$ is one of the connecting edges. This supplies a perfect matching $N$ containing $e$ with $M\cup N$ connected. The union $M'\cup N$ instead contains a separate doubled pair. Thus no two individual matchings can be identified across all exterior pairings.

For a family of attainable matchings, a completion query only asks whether any row has a one in the exterior's column. Select a basis of the attainable rows over $\mathbb F_2$, using only original rows. If a column is zero on the basis, it is zero on the entire span. If a column is one on any original row, at least one basis row also has a one there. This proves exact preservation of yes-or-no completion. It also proves safety after each join: for any successful exterior, replace its first child selection using the new representatives while fixing all other edges. Replace the second child selection with the first one's replacement now fixed. Then reduce the joined table by the same argument. The verifier uses direct binary elimination. [Bodlaender et al. (2015), rank-based representative sets](https://arxiv.org/abs/1211.1505)

The established rank of the matching-connectivity matrix is $2^{|T|/2-1}$ for positive even $|T|$. This bound applies to every attainable subfamily. It is sharp for some abstract families because the explicit basis matchings have a permutation submatrix, giving each member a private exterior query. [Cygan, Kratsch and Nederlof (2018), §3](https://doi.org/10.1145/3148227)

| Open ends | Matchings | Distinct completion rows | Maximum basis size | Smallest cover when all matchings are attainable |
| ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 2 | 1 | 1 | 1 | 1 |
| 4 | 3 | 3 | 2 | 2 |
| 6 | 15 | 15 | 4 | 4 |
| 8 | 105 | 105 | 8 | 5 |

The last column tests Boolean coverage of the complete exterior universe. Relabelling the ends lets one representative in a minimum cover be fixed to the first matching. The verifier exhausts increasing cover sizes with that row fixed. At eight ends one minimum cover is $01,23,45,67$ plus $02,13,46,57$ plus $03,14,27,56$ plus $04,16,25,37$ plus $05,17,26,34$. The five-row result concerns the complete family. A particular smaller attainable family must choose its representatives from its own rows.

## Reproduction and scope

The [verifier](verify_hc_05.py) compares two binary join orders, a balanced recursive split of sorted edges and adding one edge at a time. It runs each with full records and with basis reduction. The oracle enumerates whole-graph paths, independent of the boundary calculation. Across all 142 connected graph shapes of orders two through six, 1,933 terminal pairs gave 7,732 agreeing solver checks. The compressed runs removed 1,015 redundant rows across their intermediate tables. The seven nine-vertex HC-03 examples contributed 224 further agreeing checks, using each candidate entry and vertex $8$ as exit. The order-seven census separately tested 5,971 marked pieces against 89,565 exterior queries for the marginal minimum. [McKay's graph catalogue](https://users.cecs.anu.edu.au/~bdm/data/graphs.html)

Run from the repository root with Python 3.12. The first command checks the concrete graph examples and matching tables. The optional census uses McKay's graph6 data under CC BY 4.0, retrieved on 17 November 2026. In ascending order from two through seven the files concatenate to SHA-256 `733b70799cbeb269dc8652eda8f3d78b624d2e7fd89859e8d4dc9ee0954253f9`. [Data licence](https://users.cecs.anu.edu.au/~bdm/data/)

```sh
python3 research/hamiltonian-cycle/verify_hc_05.py

catalogues=$(mktemp -d)
for order in 2 3 4 5 6 7; do
    curl -fLsS "https://users.cecs.anu.edu.au/~bdm/data/graph${order}c.g6" \
        -o "$catalogues/graph${order}c.g6" || exit
done
python3 research/hamiltonian-cycle/verify_hc_05.py "$catalogues"/graph[2-7]c.g6
```

The verifier constructs columns for every exterior matching. The table sizes above measure state retention, with the cost of producing rows and the choice of decomposition left to a solver benchmark. The next probe is to test how representative tables grow under balanced cuts chosen for small boundaries and take advantage of the basis columns in the established algorithm. This probe was prepared with AI assistance. Priority of the small examples and complete-family cover computation has not been evaluated.
